Mission 

The Equity Research Group (ERG) is a student-run equity research organization at Northeastern University focused on under-covered small and mid-cap companies. ERG produces institutional-quality research on names that receive little Wall Street coverage, developing members into rigorous fundamental analysts. 

 

Investment Universe 

Market cap: $300M – $10B 

All sectors, all geographies (US-listed) 

No style mandate — value, growth, and GARP approaches all welcome depending on the opportunity 

 

Core Edge 

Sell-side coverage drops off dramatically below $10B market cap. ERG’s edge is simple: we do the work others don’t. This means going beyond filings and earnings calls to include customer interviews, competitor analysis, supply chain mapping, and on-the-ground research where possible. 

 

Structure 

Year One operates as a vertical leadership model. Five verticals — Technology, Industrials & Defense, Financials, Resources & Real Assets, and Consumer & Services — each have a designated Vertical Lead responsible for coordinating coverage across their sub-sectors. An eight-person executive board oversees operations, research quality, and member development. No junior analyst layer until the model is proven.

Membership 

Admission by application, stock pitch submission, and interview 

Every member pitches at least once per semester — no exceptions 

Inactive members removed at semester end 

 

Research Process 

Idea Generation 

Screens, 13F tracking, industry reading, personal observation 

Each lead maintains a watchlist of 10+ names in their sector 

Weekly sector updates surface early-stage ideas for group feedback 

 

Research Standard 

Every pitch must include: 

Business overview and industry context 

Variant perception — what is the market missing? 

Key drivers and risks 

Financial model with base, bull, and bear cases 

Valuation and price target 

Defined catalyst or timeline 

 

Pitch Format 

Written memo (15–20pages) 

30-minute presentation + 15-minute Q&A 

All leads vote: Strong Buy, Buy, , or Short 

Majority vote required for paper portfolio inclusion 

 

Portfolio Management 

Paper portfolio with $1M notional starting capital 

Position sizes: 3–7% per name at entry 

Maximum 20 positions at any time 

Positions reviewed quarterly — leads must defend or exit 

All trades logged with rationale 

 

Output 

Internal 

All memos archived in shared drive 

Portfolio tracked in real time with entry dates and prices 

Quarterly review of past calls: wins, losses, lessons learned 

 

External 

Substack publication with polished research 

Quarterly report summarizing top ideas and portfolio performance 

Goal: build a public track record members can reference in recruiting 

 

Meeting Cadence 

Weekly meetings (1.5 hours) 

One or two pitches per week, rotating through sectors 

Remaining time: sector updates, market discussion, idea generation 

Attendance mandatory; two unexcused absences triggers removal review 

 

Member Expectations 

Every member commits to: 

Minimum one full pitch per semester 

Ongoing coverage and watchlist maintenance for their sector 

Active participation in Q&A and voting 

Tracking personal calls and learning from mistakes 

 

Recruiting Positioning 

ERG exists to prepare members for equity research and investment management careers. By graduation, every member should have: 

4+ polished, interview-ready stock pitches 

Deep expertise in at least one sector 

A public track record via Substack 

Clear perspectives on markets and individual names 

 

Future: Year Two and Beyond 

Once the model is proven, ERG will add a junior analyst layer: 

Vertical leads become mentors, each managing 1–2 analysts 

Analysts complete a training program and pitch to earn voting membership 

Structure scales to 30–40 members while maintaining quality 

Analyst-to-lead promotion based on pitch quality and commitment 

 

Differentiation 

Unlike broad investment clubs that rotate members through generic projects, ERG gives each member true ownership of a coverage universe. The small-cap focus ensures original research rather than regurgitation of Street consensus. The vertical leadership structure creates clear accountability and specialization — every role has a defined owner, and there is nowhere to hide.

This section outlines the required sections, metrics, formatting standards, and quality benchmarks for all stock pitches presented to ERG. Every member must follow this checklist before submitting a pitch for presentation. Pitches that do not meet the minimum standards outlined below will be returned for revision before scheduling.

2. Business Overview & Industry Context 

What does the company do? Explain it so a non-expert understands. 

Business model: how does the company make money? 

Revenue breakdown by segment, geography, or product line (with %) 

Customer base: who pays, how concentrated, how sticky? 

End-market exposure and cyclicality 

Industry overview: size, growth rate, key trends, competitive dynamics 

Where the company sits in the value chain 

Regulatory environment (if material) 

 

Target length: 2–3 pages. Use visuals (segment breakdowns, market share charts) to replace paragraphs where possible. 

 

3. Variant Perception / Investment Thesis 

This is the most important section of your pitch. What does the market misunderstand, misprice, or ignore? If you cannot clearly articulate your variant perception, you do not have a pitch. 

 

Clearly stated variant perception — one sentence on what the market is getting wrong 

2–4 thesis points supporting the variant perception 

Each thesis point backed by evidence (data, channel checks, filings) 

Address the obvious bear case for each thesis point 

Explain why this opportunity exists (neglect, misclassification, overhang, complexity) 

Timeline: when and why should the gap close? 

 

Quality test: If your thesis is “this is a good company that will keep growing,” you do not have a variant perception. The market already knows that. What do YOU see that others don’t?  Target length: 3–4 pages. This should be the longest section of your memo.

6. Financial Model 

Every pitch requires a financial model with base, bull, and bear cases. The model should be bottom-up where possible and clearly show your assumptions. 

 

Income statement projection (minimum 3 years forward: FYE, FY+1, FY+2) 

Revenue build (bottom-up by segment, geography, or product) 

Gross profit and gross margin 

Operating expenses (ideally broken out by major line item) 

EBITDA and EBITDA margin 

Net income and EPS 

Free cash flow projection 

Net income to FCF bridge (D&A, capex, working capital) 

FCF margin and FCF conversion ratio 

Balance sheet snapshot 

Cash and debt levels 

Net debt or net cash position 

Any off-balance-sheet items or contingent liabilities 

Clearly labeled assumptions for every major line item 

Bull, base, and bear case assumptions identified 

Sensitivity analysis on 1–2 key assumptions 

 

Quality test: Could someone replicate your numbers from the assumptions you’ve stated? If not, you haven’t shown your work.  Target length: 2–3 pages in the memo. Full model should also exist in a separate spreadsheet. 

 

7. Valuation & Price Target 

Minimum two valuation methods (three preferred) 

Comparable company analysis (EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, P/E at minimum) 

DCF analysis (state WACC, terminal growth or exit multiple, and justify both) 

Scenario / probability-weighted analysis 

Comp table with 4–6 peers and relevant multiples 

Clear bridge from model output to price target 

State your implied multiple and compare to comps and history 

Sanity check: does the price target imply reasonable assumptions?

8. Management & Governance 

CEO and CFO background (prior roles, tenure, relevant experience) 

Insider ownership and recent insider transactions 

Management track record: have they delivered on past guidance? 

Capital allocation history (buybacks, dividends, M&A, reinvestment) 

Any governance red flags (dual-class shares, related-party transactions, board independence) 

Compensation structure alignment with shareholders 

 

Target length: 0.5–1 page. Brief but substantive. 

 

9. Catalysts & Timeline 

A pitch without catalysts is a hope, not a trade. Identify specific, time-bound events that will drive re-rating. 

 

Minimum 3 identifiable catalysts 

Each catalyst has a specific timeline (not “eventually”) 

Catalysts tied back to your thesis points 

Near-term catalyst (0–6 months) 

Medium-term catalyst (6–18 months) 

Explain the mechanism: how does each catalyst move the stock? 

 

Target length: 0.5–1 page. A table format works well here. 

 

10. Risks & Mitigants 

Do not bury risks or treat them as a formality. The group will push hard on risks in Q&A — you should push harder in your memo. 

 

Minimum 4–6 risks identified 

Each risk rated by probability and impact (High / Medium / Low) 

Each risk has a stated mitigant or explanation of why it’s priced in 

At least one risk that could break the thesis entirely 

Risks are specific, not generic (not “macro could slow down”) 

Steelman the bear case — what would make you wrong? 

 

Target length: 1–1.5 pages. A risk matrix table is the cleanest format. 

 

11. Appendix (Optional but Encouraged) 

Key metrics to monitor going forward (watchlist for the position) 

Detailed financial model tables (if too long for the body) 

Channel check notes or primary research summaries 

Historical valuation charts 

Ownership breakdown (institutional, insider, short interest) 

ESG or governance assessment (if material) 

 

No length limit for the appendix — include anything that strengthens the pitch. 

 

12. Formatting & Submission Standards 

Total memo length: 10–15 pages (excluding appendix) 

File format: PDF (submitted) + editable Word doc or Google Doc 

Accompanying Excel model (separate file) 

All data sourced: SEC filings, company reports, or clearly labeled estimates 

Tables and charts labeled with source and date 

Consistent font, spacing, and formatting throughout 

No copy-paste from sell-side reports or other research 

Proofread — typos and errors undermine credibility

13. Presentation Standards 

30-minute presentation (hard stop) 

15-minute Q&A to follow 

Slide deck optional but recommended (max 20 slides) 

Memo distributed to all members minimum 24 hours before presentation 

Know your model cold — you will be asked to walk through assumptions live 

Prepare for adversarial questions on your weakest thesis point 

Have a clear recommendation and conviction level 

 

14. Voting & Portfolio Inclusion 

After Q&A, all sector leads vote on the pitch: 

 

Strong Buy — high conviction, add to portfolio at 5–7% position size 

Buy — add to portfolio at 3–5% position size 

Hold — interesting but not enough conviction for a position 

Short — the stock should go down; add short position if applicable 

 

Majority vote required for paper portfolio inclusion. Ties resolved by VP/CIO. 

 

Final note: This checklist is a minimum standard, not a ceiling. The best pitches will go beyond these requirements with original primary research, creative analysis, and deep sector expertise. Your pitch is your reputation in this group — make it count.
