## Equity Research Group

### Mission
The Equity Research Group (ERG) is a student-run equity research organization at Northeastern University focused on under-covered small and mid-cap companies. ERG produces institutional-quality research on names that receive little Wall Street coverage, developing members into rigorous fundamental analysts.

---

## Investment Universe

- Market cap: $300M-$10B
- All sectors, all geographies, limited to US-listed companies
- No style mandate; value, growth, and GARP approaches are all welcome depending on the opportunity

---

## Core Edge

Sell-side coverage drops off dramatically below $10B market cap. ERG's edge is simple: the group does the work others do not.

Research should go beyond filings and earnings calls where possible, including:

- customer interviews
- competitor analysis
- supply chain mapping
- on-the-ground research

## Research Process

### Idea Generation

- Screens
- 13F tracking
- Industry reading
- Personal observation

Each lead maintains a watchlist of 10+ names in their sector.

Weekly sector updates surface early-stage ideas for group feedback.

### Research Standard

Every pitch must include:

- business overview and industry context
- variant perception: what is the market missing?
- key drivers and risks
- financial model with base, bull, and bear cases
- valuation and price target
- defined catalyst or timeline

### Pitch Format

- Written memo
- 30-minute presentation plus 15-minute Q&A
- All leads vote: Strong Buy, Buy, Hold, or Short
- Majority vote required for paper portfolio inclusion

## Stock Pitch Standards

Every stock pitch must meet the checklist below before presentation. Pitches that do not meet the minimum standard should be returned for revision before scheduling.

### 1. Cover Page & One-Page Summary

Include:

- company name, ticker, exchange, and current share price
- recommendation: Strong Buy, Buy, Hold, or Short
- price target and implied upside/downside
- market cap and enterprise value
- analyst name, sector, and date
- 3-year summary financial table
- one-paragraph thesis summary

Required summary KPIs:

- Revenue: FY-1A, FYE, FY+1E
- EPS: FY-1A, FYE, FY+1E
- EV/Revenue: current vs. peer average
- EV/EBITDA: current vs. peer average
- P/E: current and forward
- FCF Yield: current

Target length: 1 page.

### 2. Business Overview & Industry Context

Include:

- business model
- revenue breakdown by segment, geography, or product line
- customer base, concentration, and stickiness
- end-market exposure and cyclicality
- industry size, growth, key trends, and competitive dynamics
- value chain position
- regulatory environment if material

Target length: 2-3 pages.

### 3. Variant Perception / Investment Thesis

Include:

- one-sentence variant perception
- 2-4 thesis points supporting the variant perception
- evidence for each thesis point
- the obvious bear case for each thesis point
- explanation of why the opportunity exists
- timeline for when and why the gap should close

Target length: 3-4 pages.

### 4. Competitive Positioning & Moat Analysis

Include:

- 3-5 direct competitors
- competitive comparison table
- moat assessment
- switching costs, network effects, scale advantages, intangible assets, efficient scale, or other relevant barriers
- customer retention or churn metrics where relevant
- disruption or substitution threats

Required comparison metrics:

- revenue growth
- gross margin
- EBITDA margin
- market cap / enterprise value
- key differentiator

Target length: 1-2 pages.

### 5. Key Drivers & Operating KPIs

Include:

- the 3-5 metrics that matter most for the business
- quarterly or annual progression for at least 6-8 periods
- explanation of trends and forward expectations
- inflection points or trajectory changes

Target length: 1-2 pages.

### 6. Financial Model

Include:

- income statement projection for at least 3 years forward
- bottom-up revenue build where possible
- gross profit and gross margin
- operating expenses by major line item where possible
- EBITDA and EBITDA margin
- net income and EPS
- free cash flow projection
- net income to FCF bridge
- balance sheet snapshot
- cash, debt, net debt, or net cash position
- clearly labeled assumptions
- bull, base, and bear cases
- sensitivity analysis on 1-2 key assumptions

Target length in memo: 2-3 pages. Full model should also exist separately.

### 7. Valuation & Price Target

Include at least two valuation methods, with three preferred:

- comparable company analysis
- DCF analysis
- scenario or probability-weighted analysis
- comp table with 4-6 peers
- clear bridge from model output to price target
- implied multiple compared to comps and history
- sanity check on implied assumptions

Required valuation KPIs:

- EV/Revenue
- EV/EBITDA
- P/E
- FCF Yield
- WACC
- implied upside/downside by scenario

Target length: 2-3 pages.

### 8. Management & Governance

Include:

- CEO and CFO background
- insider ownership and recent insider transactions
- management track record
- capital allocation history
- governance red flags
- compensation alignment

Target length: 0.5-1 page.

### 9. Catalysts & Timeline

Include:

- at least 3 identifiable catalysts
- specific timelines
- catalysts tied back to thesis points
- near-term catalyst within 0-6 months
- medium-term catalyst within 6-18 months
- explanation of how each catalyst moves the stock

Target length: 0.5-1 page.

### 10. Risks & Mitigants

Include:

- 4-6 specific risks
- probability and impact rating for each risk
- mitigant or explanation of why the risk is priced in
- at least one risk that could break the thesis entirely
- steelman bear case

Target length: 1-1.5 pages.

### 11. Appendix

Optional but encouraged:

- key metrics to monitor
- detailed model tables
- channel check notes or primary research summaries
- historical valuation charts
- ownership breakdown
- ESG or governance assessment where material

### 12. Formatting & Submission Standards

- Total memo length: 10-15 pages, excluding appendix
- File format: submitted PDF plus editable Word document or Google Doc
- Accompanying Excel model
- All data sourced from SEC filings, company reports, or clearly labeled estimates
- Tables and charts labeled with source and date
- Consistent font, spacing, and formatting
- No copy-paste from sell-side reports or other research
- Proofread before submission

### 13. Presentation Standards

- 30-minute presentation, hard stop
- 15-minute Q&A
- Slide deck optional but recommended, max 20 slides
- Memo distributed to all members at least 24 hours before presentation
- Analyst must know the model cold
- Analyst must prepare for adversarial questions on the weakest thesis point
- Recommendation and conviction level must be clear

### 14. Voting & Portfolio Inclusion

After Q&A, all sector leads vote:

- Strong Buy: high conviction, add to portfolio at 5-7% position size
- Buy: add to portfolio at 3-5% position size
- Hold: interesting but not enough conviction for a position
- Short: stock should go down; add short position if applicable

Majority vote is required for paper portfolio inclusion. Ties are resolved by the Executive VP / CIO function.

## Portfolio Management

- Paper portfolio with $1M notional starting capital
- Position sizes: 3-7% per name at entry
- Maximum 20 positions at any time
- Positions reviewed quarterly; leads must defend or exit
- All trades logged with rationale

## Output

### Internal

- All memos archived in shared drive
- Portfolio tracked in real time with entry dates and prices
- Quarterly review of past calls: wins, losses, lessons learned

### External

- Substack publication with polished research
- Quarterly report summarizing top ideas and portfolio performance

### Goal

Build a public track record members can reference in recruiting.

---

## Meeting Cadence

- Weekly meetings of 1.5 hours
- One or two pitches per week, rotating through sectors
- Remaining time for sector updates, market discussion, and idea generation
- Attendance mandatory; two unexcused absences trigger removal review

---

## Member Expectations

Every member commits to:

- minimum one full pitch per semester
- ongoing coverage and watchlist maintenance for their sector
- active participation in Q&A and voting
- tracking personal calls and learning from mistakes

---

## Recruiting Positioning

ERG exists to prepare members for equity research and investment management careers. By graduation, every member should have:

- 4+ polished, interview-ready stock pitches
- deep expertise in at least one sector
- public track record via Substack
- clear perspectives on markets and individual names

---

## Future: Year Two and Beyond

Once the model is proven, ERG will add a junior analyst layer:

- vertical leads become mentors, each managing 1-2 analysts
- analysts complete a training program and pitch to earn voting membership
- structure scales to 30-40 members while maintaining quality
- analyst-to-lead promotion is based on pitch quality and commitment

---

## Differentiation

Unlike broad investment clubs that rotate members through generic projects, ERG gives each member true ownership of a coverage universe.

The small-cap focus ensures original research rather than regurgitation of Street consensus.

The vertical leadership structure creates clear accountability and specialization: every role has a defined owner, and there is nowhere to hide.
